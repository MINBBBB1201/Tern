## 1. Product Hunt

**Tagline (≤60 chars):**
> Compare flights by price, speed, delay risk & points

*(51 chars. Alternate: "Flight search that shows the trade-offs, not just price" — 54 chars.)*

**Maker description:**

> Every flight search forces trade-offs — the cheapest fare is rarely the
> fastest, and the fastest isn't always the one that actually lands on time.
> Most tools hide that behind a single sorted list. Tern puts it in the open.
>
> One search returns five picks side by side — Cheapest, Fastest, Earliest
> arrival, an AI-balanced pick, and Lowest delay risk — and shows what each fare

> connections) and is labeled as an estimate — not fake "live" data.
>
> No countdown timers, no "3 seats left," no invented coupons, no sponsored
> ranking. Tern is free, shows no ads, and never sells your data; it earns a
> small commission only if you book through a partner, at no extra cost — and
> that's spelled out on a "How we make money" page rather than buried.
>
> Would love feedback on the delay-risk signal and the points-vs-cash view.

## 2. Hacker News — Show HN

**Title:**
> Show HN: Tern – flight search that shows delay risk and points value, honestly

**First comment:**

> I built Tern because flight search UIs quietly optimize for one number (usually
> price) and hide the trade-offs. One Tern search returns five picks side by
> side — Cheapest, Fastest, Earliest arrival, an AI-balanced pick, and Lowest
> delay risk — and shows what each fare would cost in transferred card points
> next to the cash price.
>
> On the delay-risk score specifically: it's a relative heuristic computed from
> the itinerary's shape — number of stops, connection buffers, red-eye timing —
> and it's labeled in the UI as an estimate. It is NOT live ATC/flight-status
> data, and I've been careful not to present it as if it were. If that framing
> is still misleading, I'd genuinely like to know.
>
> A few deliberate "won'ts": no fake urgency ("3 seats left"), no invented
> deals/coupons, no sponsored ranking, no fabricated stats or review counts.
> Promotions just link to the airline's own pages. It's free and ad-free; it
> earns a small affiliate commission only when you book through a partner (at no
> extra cost), and that model is stated on a "How we make money" page rather than
> buried in a footer.
>
> Stack: Next.js (App Router) with live fares from Duffel, a White Label booking
> path, server-rendered structured data + sitemap, and i18n in four languages
> (EN/JA/KO/ZH). Airport guides are reshaped from real data, not generated fluff.
>
> Happy to answer anything about the ranking logic, the risk heuristic, or the
> honesty constraints I put on myself. Feedback welcome.
